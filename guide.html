Getting here

There is no airport on Koh Phangan. Every route ends with a boat, and the last boat of the day is earlier than you think.

FromHowRoughly
Koh SamuiFast ferry from Nathon, Maenam or Big Buddha pier to Thong Sala20–40 min. Most crossings of the day.
Koh TaoFast ferry, usually continuing to SamuiAbout 1.5 hours
Surat Thani & DonsakCar ferry or fast boat from Donsak pier2.5–4.5 hours depending on the boat
BangkokFlight to Samui, or overnight bus/train to Surat Thani, then the boatHalf a day either way
Book the boat, not just the flight. Around full moon, New Year and Songkran, ferries sell out and prices on the last crossings climb. Arriving in Surat Thani at 9pm with no boat booked means a night on the mainland.

Getting around

The island is about 20km across, the main roads are good, and a few of the roads that matter are genuinely dangerous.

Scooter

Cheap, and the main way people get hurt here

Wear the helmet — it's the law and it's also the difference between a scare and a hospital. Photograph the bike from every angle before you ride off. Take the insurance question seriously: most travel policies don't cover you without the right licence.

Roads to respect

Haad Rin hill, Bottle Beach, Haad Yuan

The hill into Haad Rin and the tracks to the far beaches are steep, loose and take scooters down every week. If you're not confident, take a taxi or a boat. There's no shame in it and the locals will agree with you.

Taxis

Shared pickups, fixed-ish prices

Songthaews run set routes from Thong Sala and charge per person. Agree the price before you get in. Late at night, after the pier and the parties close, expect to pay several times the daytime rate.

Boats

The only sane way to reach some beaches

Longtails run to Bottle Beach, Haad Yuan and Haad Thian in calm weather. In the windy months they simply don't go, and no amount of asking changes that.

Walks and waterfalls

Most of the island's interior is national park, and almost nobody walks it. Go early, take twice the water you think you need, and tell someone where you went.

Half a day · hard

Khao Ra, the island's high point

A steep climb through jungle to about 630m, with a view over the whole island and out to Samui on a clear morning. Two hours up if you're fit. Start at dawn — by 9am the heat makes it a different walk. Guided groups go most weeks.

2 hours · moderate

Bottle Beach over the headland

The trail from Chaloklum climbs and drops over the ridge to one of the last beaches with no road to it. Sturdy shoes, not flip-flops. Most people walk one way and take the longtail back.

1–2 hours · easy

Phaeng waterfall & Domsila viewpoint

The easiest waterfall to reach, in the middle of the island, with a marked path continuing up to a viewpoint over the west coast. The falls are worth it in the wet season and a trickle in the dry one.

Half a day · moderate

Than Sadet, on the east coast

A river running down through boulders to the sea, with pools you can sit in and rocks carved by generations of Thai kings. The road in is steep; the walk up the river is the good part.

Two things that go wrong on these walks. People run out of water, and people set off at 11am. The jungle holds the heat and there is no shop at the top of anything. Also: several trails sit inside national park land, where a ranger may charge an entry fee — carry some cash.

Beaches worth the ride

Mae Haad & Koh Ma

A sandbar you can walk across to a small island at low tide, and the best snorkelling on Koh Phangan around its edge. Go on a rising tide and bring your own mask.

Bottle Beach

No road, a handful of bungalows, and the feeling of the island thirty years ago. Boat from Chaloklum, or walk the headland.

Secret Beach & Haad Son

West coast, so the sunsets land here. Small, rocky at the edges, and busy at 6pm for exactly that reason.

Zen Beach

Not really a beach, more a ritual. Everyone gathers for sunset, someone brings drums, someone brings fire. It's the most Koh Phangan hour of the week.

Haad Yuan & Haad Thian

Reachable by boat from Haad Rin, or a hard track. Quiet bays with their own small scenes.

Thong Nai Pan Noi

The classic curve of pale sand in the northeast. Swimmable most of the year, and far enough that it never feels crowded.

The rules that catch people out

None of this is dramatic. It's the short list of things that turn a good trip into a police station, and most visitors arrive with out-of-date information about at least one of them.

1

Cannabis is medical-only now, whatever you read in 2023

Thailand decriminalised cannabis in 2022, then reversed course. Since June 2025 cannabis flower is a controlled herb: buying it legally requires a prescription from a Thai-licensed practitioner, issued after a real consultation. A medical card from your own country doesn't count. Rules tightened again in 2026. Smoking in public is separately prohibited, and taking any of it across a border — in either direction, on any flight — is a serious offence. Shops that sell you flower without the paperwork are breaking the rules, not proving they don't exist.

2

Vapes and e-cigarettes are illegal in Thailand

Not restricted — illegal to import, sell or possess. Enforcement is inconsistent, which is exactly what makes people careless. Fines and confiscation are the common outcome.

3

Helmets, licences and the roadside stop

Helmets are required. Riding without one, or without a licence that actually covers a motorbike, is the most common fine on the island — and the reason an insurance claim gets refused after an accident.

4

Temples, and what to wear at them

Shoulders and knees covered, shoes off where the signs say so, and don't climb on or pose in front of Buddha images. Wat Phu Khao Noi and the forest temples are working religious sites, not backdrops.

5

National park fees and marine park rules

Some waterfalls and viewpoints sit in protected land with an entry fee for foreign visitors. Around the reefs, standing on coral and feeding fish are prohibited. Bring cash; card is rarely an option at a ranger hut.

6

Drinks you didn't watch being poured

At the big parties, buckets are strong, cheap and easy to lose track of. Watch your drink being made, don't leave it, and swim later rather than after. The sea around Haad Rin at 3am has taken people.

This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice. Thai law around cannabis in particular has changed several times in three years and is still moving. Check the Ministry of Public Health or your embassy for anything you're going to act on.

Money, weather and getting looked after

Money

Bring cash, expect ATM fees

Foreign-card ATM withdrawals carry a fixed fee per withdrawal, so take out larger amounts less often. Plenty of restaurants, bars and every market stall are cash only, including some that look like they wouldn't be.

Seasons

The wet months are real

Roughly October to December brings the heaviest rain and the roughest sea, when boats to the outer beaches stop and some places close. The rest of the year is hot, with short storms. High season and the best diving visibility run through the first months of the year.

Health

Clinics here, hospital on Samui

There are clinics and a hospital in Thong Sala for everyday problems — infected cuts, stomach trouble, scooter road rash. Anything serious goes to Samui, which means a boat. Travel insurance is not optional on this island.

Bites & stings

Mosquitoes, sandflies, jellyfish

Dengue exists here, so repellent in the evening is worth the habit. Sandflies on some beaches leave itchy bites for a week. Jellyfish appear seasonally — if there's vinegar on a post at the beach, there's a reason.

The party calendar, for people who want to plan around it

The Full Moon Party at Haad Rin is the island's biggest night, and the island reorganises itself around it. Rooms cost more, ferries fill, and the whole south coast is awake until sunrise.

In the days either side there are half-moon, jungle and beach parties in Ban Tai and the inland venues. If that's why you came, book the ferry and the room a month ahead. If it isn't, the north of the island barely notices — Chaloklum, Thong Nai Pan and Mae Haad stay completely quiet, and you can happily spend a full moon there without hearing a thing.

Dates move. Parties follow the lunar calendar and shift for Buddhist holidays, when alcohol sales are restricted island-wide. Check the dates for your month rather than assuming.
